Task-Specific Manipulability Metrics for Redundancy Optimization in Cooperative Manipulation

IITGN Robotics Laboratory, Department of Mechanical Engineering, Indian Institute of Technology Gandhinagar, Gandhinagar, Gujarat 382355, India

Abstract

Purpose — This paper investigates how redundant degrees of freedom can be used in cooperative manipulation to improve task-relevant dexterity or force capability without disturbing the commanded object motion. The aim is to compare task-specific manipulability metrics for cooperative robotic grasping, with particular attention to external loads whose direction may differ from the desired motion direction.

Design/methodology/approach — An established closed-loop inverse-kinematics and null-space gradient-projection structure is used as a common implementation basis. Three objectives are evaluated: velocity manipulability, force manipulability, and directional-force manipulability. The objectives are compared on a planar dual-arm hardware platform with pin-jointed object coupling, external load trials, and actuator-current-based effort measurement. A dual-Franka 7-DoF simulation studies the scalability of the velocity-manipulability implementation to spatial bimanual manipulation.

Findings — Hardware experiments show that the objectives produce distinct internal joint configurations while preserving object motion. Velocity manipulability improves kinematic dexterity during holding, translation, and rotation. Force manipulability reduces actuator-current effort when the load direction is aligned with the main force requirement. When motion and external-load directions differ, directional-force manipulability achieves lower effort than force-only optimization. The dual-Franka simulation shows that velocity-manipulability optimization improves or maintains manipulability without degrading object tracking.

Originality/value — The paper provides an implementation-oriented comparison of velocity, force, and directional-force manipulability objectives for cooperative manipulation, offering practical guidance for selecting redundancy objectives according to task condition and external load direction.
